# La Catrina — real component and material basis

Researched 2026-11-06. This document separates the user's visual design from verified product construction. The original sheet is `references/La-Catrina-Master.jpg`. Its dimensions, plum interior, pink/champagne decoration, custom rear spring saddles and hydraulic choreography are the art brief. They are not evidence of a specific commercially available vehicle or a certified suspension conversion.

The mesh remains original game geometry. No manufacturer CAD or product imagery is embedded in it. The roof, hood and deck paint maps are rectified from the user's reference sheet, and the side scroll uses a mask extracted from the same sheet; their provenance is documented in [REFERENCE-PAINT.md](REFERENCE-PAINT.md). Source links below establish component types and selected proportions; they do not establish safe installation, load capacity or exact production dimensions for this fictional car.

## Baseline findings and repairs

The baseline `scripts/catrina_generate.py` already had the correct component families: an X frame, one linked rear axle, separate wheel/steering groups, two pump circuits, coils and moving hydraulic cups. `showcase/CatrinaRig.hpp` kept steel link lengths and rear axle span constant. Those constraints are retained.

The initial front arms were narrow triangular tubes with parallel equal-length kinematics. `scripts/catrina_hardware.py` now replaces their simple tube appearance with stamped-channel wishbones, bush sleeves, cross shafts, boots and fasteners. It does **not** claim to correct the simplified factory camber curve.

The baseline had no separate telescoping shocks. The helper adds four dampers with frame brackets and lower-arm clevises. Front attachments remain at 38% of arm span, with a 30 mm local vertical offset to keep the lower spherical bearing clear of the formed arm. A later cabin inspection found that the original rear towers penetrated the passenger space. Their replacements sit below the trunk: upper eyes at X ±0.20, Y 0.50, Z −2.00 m; lower eyes at 84% of the existing trailing-arm span with a 100 mm inboard bracket. The rig rotates each fixed body between its eyes and slides a rigid shaft. The crossmember, short upper supports and lower brackets are physical geometry, not hidden old towers.

These rear shocks use custom 0.665 m barrel-end distance and 0.660 m shafts. This long-stroke packaging is **not the referenced FOX 12-inch-travel product or an exact commercial SKU**. Catalog references support their smooth-body construction, shaft/casing diameters, eyes and seals; the new lengths are authored for the game's large axle travel. A numeric sweep reconstructed from all 243 exported poses gives rear eye distances of 0.71237–1.28154 m, at least 43.46 mm rod engagement and 17.37 mm upper-head clearance. Conservative centerline capsules also clear both rear tires and the rear bulkhead, and retain about 19.9 mm separation from the coil envelopes in the sampled configurations. Those source-level checks precede rebuilt native and serialized-asset verification.

The spring wire now has continuous normals instead of separate six-sided cylinder segments. Formed upper cups and independent lower seats remain rigid; lower seats follow the solved spring bottoms rather than inheriting the coil's vertical scale. Brake drums, hub flanges, studs and grease caps rotate in separate brake groups, allowing wheel-off service views to retain them while the backing plates stay stationary. These are authored component shapes, not manufacturer meshes or a brake-performance simulation.

The hydraulic assembly uses the actual final diameters listed above. Fixed ram tops remain at 0.86 m, with the rest cup at 0.55 m. A later mechanical review found that the old 360 mm casing enveloped the rest cup and that the old commanded stroke could drive that cup below the lower spring seat. The replacement uses a rigid 245 mm shaft, a 245 mm pivot-to-gland distance and a cup-distance range of 280-430 mm. The final spherical upper mount puts the casing skin 30-245 mm below the pivot, making the actual shell 215 mm long. Reinforced posts and open header rings connect each top bearing to the inboard frame-rail web; relieved wheelhouse pockets and lined floor openings avoid hiding a steel member through a body skin. The visible cup stops when its signed distance to the lower seat reaches 114 mm, leaving 12 mm above the six-turn, 17 mm-wire solid-height estimate. The physical simulation's requested pressure/preload is unchanged; this deliberate presentation approximation is documented in [RIG.md](RIG.md). A 243-pose source reconstruction at three command levels found at least 60 mm hydraulic shaft engagement, 21.98 mm cup-to-gland clearance and 10.98 mm upper-head clearance. These are source checks, not a replacement for rebuilt native and serialized verification.

Another review found damper pins rotating away from their fixed fork axes and a steel propshaft stretching with axle movement. Each damper now has fixed-axis pins, inner spherical bearings and spacers that follow the frame or lower arm; its outer races follow the barrel and shaft directions. Short rigid necks connect upper races to their barrels. Paired fork-side supports replace the obstructing central towers; rear lower fork webs lead forward to offset arm saddles, leaving the bearing centers and aft-pointing shafts in open pockets. The source sweep gives 28.43 degrees maximum bearing articulation against the authored 30-degree limit. The rear propshaft now uses two rigid members, with only their overlap changing. Source-reconstructed joint separation is 1.81043-1.88207 m, leaving at least 267.9 mm slip-member engagement. Native checks inspect actual loaded witness transforms, world scales, centers and axes. The bay retains separate reservoir caps, check valves, dump solenoids, hose crimps, electrical contactors and motor tie bolts.

Round-02 rendered views exposed a substantial installation error: the axle/differential and ram towers were visible in the load space, inner quarter panels appeared open, and the batteries obscured the pumps. Round 03 replaces the low service floor with a closed lined basin, raised central floor over the differential, full-width aft floor, closed wheel housings and capped ram pockets. Four retained batteries move to the forward equipment row; two polished motor/pump/reservoir assemblies sit on a separate raised aft rack. The engine bay also receives inner fenders, wheel housings and a radiator support. These skins require the separate native-pose tire-clearance check and fresh rendered inspection; merely adding them is not evidence of visual acceptance.

The first native-pose enclosure check found genuine interference in the old rear cabin tunnel and the new rectangular front inner-fender walls. The subsequent repair narrows the tunnel to 0.84 m and cuts the inner-fender lower edge to follow the wheelhouse crown. Sampling those replacement surfaces against all 972 exported wheel transforms, including the unchanged 6 mm tire allowance, produced no intersections: the minimum cylinder metrics were 36.2 mm and 98.0 mm respectively. This targeted in-memory result precedes the final serialized-asset check. Service images also revealed that smooth normals made the old four-sided rail tubes look rounded. The helper now authors ten broad-web boxed rail segments, eight boxed outriggers attached to the actual rail locations, rectangular crossmembers, junction plates and body mounting bushes. All existing moving-joint pivots remain unchanged.

The helper's source-level geometry check produces finite vertices with full parent rotation/translation/scale. The native 243-pose audit checks damper engagement, head clearance, pin centers/axes and bearing articulation; its final result must come from the rebuilt native scene with the matching serialized asset.

## Review limits that remain explicit

- The front visual solver uses equal-length parallel arms. Native wheel contact queries are straight suspension rays, while visible linkages follow arcs. This is a reduced game rig, not a full multibody suspension solver.
- Hydraulic pressure, fluid temperature, seal behavior, structural fatigue, tire load capacity and exact damping curves are not simulated or certified by these component references.
- The extreme-travel check establishes link closure and rod engagement, not exhaustive moving-mesh collision clearance. Review wheel wells, driveline, hose slack and seat/cup contact in the actual hopping sequence.
- The reference's artwork, frame dimensions, custom axle saddles, upholstery color and cinematic hop amplitude remain artistic specifications. Manufacturer product pages do not validate those design choices.

Search discovery used the fleet SearXNG endpoint first. Known pages and manufacturer PDFs were then read directly. The GM scan and Global West page were discovery-only evidence in this pass, and are explicitly not used for unverified measurements.
