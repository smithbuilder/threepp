# Independent lighting and rendering reassessment

November 9, 2026, Pacific Time. Evaluator did not implement the reviewed rendering, importer, city or materials. Review used existing pixels and source, with no editor or player launches and no runtime or asset edits. Previous review scores were not used as a target or baseline.

**Verdict: r9 is a substantial visual regression from the native reference.** The car identities survive, but the materials, grounding, facade depth and lighting hierarchy do not. This is a rendering and environment adaptation gap, not evidence that the simulation needs replacing or that Built-in forward is incapable of the intended result. A probe pass establishes neither fidelity nor visual acceptance.

## What is present, and what actually went missing

### P1: Restore the native city's authored lighting/material data, not just its brick photographs

**Proven:** Unity spawns a replacement `city-kit` authored by `tools/blender/cinder/create_kit.py:85-135`. It is not an export of `showcase/CityBlock.hpp`. The staging manifest explicitly calls it an original adaptation rather than identical donor art. Native facade geometry is constructed around real openings with recesses, varied blinds/curtains, stone, awnings, fire escapes and grime/material variation. The replacement windows and ground-floor geometry are a materially simpler construction. Lighting cannot restore missing geometry or surface variety.

Native `CityBlock.hpp:37-44` multiplies masonry foot AO, `city::shade`, surface variation and foliage vertex colours into static mesh colour. `CityLayout.hpp:44-65` supplies long building shadows at 0.52 brightness plus tree canopy and ground-contact darkening. Native ground is also partitioned and coloured using this shadow function (`CityBlock.hpp:191-202`). Unity `CinderView.cs:39-45` instead gives city ground vertex colour 1 and relies on its realtime sun. None of the nine nonfoliage primitives in the staged Unity city GLB has a real `COLOR_0` accessor. This is missing authored data in the replacement asset, not the importer dropping existing wall AO.

Native uses colour, normal and spatially varying roughness for asphalt, masonry, bark, roof and repair patches, plus paving normals/roughness (`CityBlock.hpp:175-190`). Unity staging copies nine selected city images and no roughness images (`tools/unity/stage_assets.py:60-62`). Build assigns masonry normals but leaves their generated smoothness at 0.5, versus native roughness about 0.93-0.94. Runtime ground assigns asphalt albedo and constant smoothness 0.13; the copied asphalt normal is never assigned. Paving similarly has colour and constant smoothness, without native normal/roughness maps. Bark/trim/planters remain flat materials. These are direct, finite losses.

**Restore:** export or faithfully reconstruct the native static city geometry and its baked shade/AO attributes, with original material identities, UV density and roughness/normal maps. Preserve the authoritative contact mesh and simulation. Start with one exact Mercer corner and its two visible buildings, street, tree and car before extending coverage. Keep static batching. Do not try to recover recesses and street age by increasing ambient intensity.

### P1: Make shadowing and contact coherent across the street and hero car

**Proven:** runtime `StandaloneHost.cs:53` forces shadow distance 18 m, Low resolution, zero cascades and no MSAA for ordinary play as well as captures. Sun custom resolution is 512 (`:39`). The serialized profile named Ultra is therefore misleading as a quality indicator; runtime overrides dominate. Native also uses a bounded 512 vehicle shadow map, but supplements it with the static shadow field described above. Unity removed that supplement while asking the realtime map to carry the scene.

The r9 stills show a broad dark road area beside very bright pavement and thin, noisy lit trim, without the same nuanced contact/occlusion as native. Existing captures do not isolate shadow acne, detached contact and specular aliasing from each other. Do not label every bright edge a flipped normal or cure it by disabling shadows.

No lightmap bake, light-probe layout, SSAO or dedicated contact-shadow implementation is built by the inspected runtime path. Native is not using a full GI bake either, so “add GI” is not itself the missing-port diagnosis. Restoring native static shade first is a cheaper and more directly evidenced target. Then test a small calibrated car-local shadow setup for tyre/chassis contact, with bias and receiver checks, within existing safety caps. A wider/higher-resolution evaluation profile requires its separate authorization.

### P1: Restore coherent, filtered reflection lighting before retuning paint colours

**Proven:** `CinderBuild.cs:24` imports a separate custom reflection cube with default maximum 128. Its `.meta` has `cubemapConvolution: 0`, ordinary mipmaps and `seamlessCubemap: 0`. No explicit specular convolution or probe bake is performed in the build/runtime code. Body clearcoat calls `Unity_GlossyEnvironment`, which expects a meaningful roughness-dependent environment. Glass samples explicit cube mip levels. Ordinary downsampled image mips are not a verified replacement for the native roughness-filtered environment pipeline.

The sky shader is rotated 195.35 degrees, while no corresponding rotation is applied to the custom reflection cube (`StandaloneHost.cs:42`). Native applies `city::skyRotation` to both environment and visible sky (`suspension_playground.cpp:103-106`). Thus Unity's reflection direction is not explicitly tied to its visible sun/sky direction. It uses global sky-only reflections, with no local shop/street probe, while the native studio references use deliberately constructed PMREM reflection rooms. A studio-to-street comparison cannot establish that a local city probe is mandatory, but matching the original studio is necessary for a fair paint check.

**Restore:** use a validated specular-convolved HDR cube, consistent sky/reflection orientation and exposure, and recorded roughness mip behavior. First compare a grey/chrome/roughness sweep and the unchanged car under an equivalent studio environment. Then compare on the city corner. Increase cube detail only after filtering and orientation are correct; do not spend the frame budget on an unfiltered larger cube.

### P1: Calibrate the complete linear-light/output chain against native

**Proven:** Unity is Linear, Built-in forward and D3D11 (`CinderBuild.cs:19-20`, GraphicsSettings). The host camera explicitly enables HDR and attaches `CinderGrade` (`StandaloneHost.cs:38`). It is incorrect to say r9 lacks HDR, tone mapping or a camera post effect.

However, the output transforms differ. Native `src/shaders/ShaderChunk/tonemapping_pars_fragment.glsl:35-72` uses ACES input/output matrices, RRT/ODT fit and exposure divided by 0.6. Its `OutputPass` then performs output colour encoding. Unity `CinderGrade.shader:9-10` uses a per-channel rational fit with a 0.98 multiplier and `saturate`. Both being called ACES-like does not make the exposure, gamut or highlight behaviour equivalent. Sun colours/intensity, ambient model and environment intensity also differ: Unity warm sun 2.4 plus fixed Trilight, versus native city sun 2.9, low hemisphere fill and environment 0.65. Those numeric intensities cannot be transplanted blindly between engines.

The bright pavement/trim and dark blue car side are observed. **Their exact proportions caused by exposure, material smoothness, shadowing and colour transform remain unisolated.** The capture target is ARGB32 (`StandaloneHost.cs:44`); this alone does not prove that HDR was clipped before `OnRenderImage`. Inspect the actual pre-grade texture format and floating-point values before asserting that failure.

**Restore:** one documented conversion chain with albedo/data textures correctly tagged, a neutral grey/white/black and saturated-colour chart, reflective test objects and raw float pre-grade diagnostics. Match shadowed and sunlit material readability, not a histogram alone. Compare the current grade to a native-equivalent transform under unchanged lighting, then tune lighting under the selected transform. Do not compensate for a wrong output transform by repainting the cars.

## P2: Specific material/importer and edge-quality corrections

1. **Foliage variation is actually dropped.** The staged city leaf primitive's `COLOR_0` is all white; its `COLOR_1` holds the authored 0.6800-1.0 RGB variation. `GlbImport.cs:19,65` only reads `COLOR_0`. Preserve the intended colour channel explicitly during author/export. `CinderFoliage.shader` is a manual fixed-direction brightness shader with no received shadow, ambient or fog integration and no shadow-caster pass. This materially differs from the native canopy material and per-leaf occlusion. Restore integrated, inexpensive foliage light/shadow response after the major street corrections.
2. **Car vertex colours and mesh normals are not generally missing.** `GlbImport.cs:63-68` imports supplied normals, `COLOR_0`, indices and recalculated tangents; Body multiplies vertex RGB into albedo. Staged Catrina has 98 of 242 primitives with real `COLOR_0`; Nocturne has none in this staged source. Neither inspected car contains an occlusion texture, so the importer's lack of an occlusionTexture field is not an established explanation for these specific cars. There are no real source TANGENT accessors in these three staged kits; recalculation is necessary, rather than evidence of lost authored tangents. A local triangle-cross-product versus supplied-normal check found overwhelmingly consistent source orientation, not wholesale inside-out geometry. Do not apply a global winding reversal. Test tangent-space handedness/normal-green direction using a calibration patch and the actual normal maps before changing them.
3. **The car material adaptation changes donor response.** `RefreshCoat` clamps coat roughness to at least 0.10, limits coated normal strength to 0.12 and high-metal smoothness to at most 0.86. The custom Body shader is Standard plus an added coat lobe, rather than the native MeshPhysicalMaterial implementation. Those changes are real, but their visual contribution needs a same-car lighting control. Validate coat-off, Standard-only and current-coat renders with a roughness sweep. Check generated shader reflection coordinates with normal mapping enabled before diagnosing a space mismatch; source alone does not establish that bug.
4. **Glass is an artistic approximation.** Car glass ignores the normal map and metallic properties it exposes, clamps decoded reflection to 2 and alpha-blends a fixed reflection/tint mix. ShopGlass is opaque, ignores imported vertex tint/material roughness and applies fixed darkening, Fresnel and reflection clamp (`CinderGlass.shader`, `CinderShopGlass.shader`). This explains why restoring Standard parameters alone will not restore the panes. Judge transmission, cabin readability and street-pane variation at front, side and grazing angles; keep glass conservatively bounded and avoid introducing expensive screen-space effects as the first fix.
5. **AA exists but is limited.** MSAA is forced off, yet the attached Grade effect performs a directional FXAA-like filter after its tone operation. The native also uses FXAA after OutputPass, so “native has AA, Unity has none” is false. Static r9 images visibly retain sparkling thin chrome/roof/lettering edges. A still cannot measure temporal shimmer; use a bounded slow camera move at the existing capture profile. Test highlight filtering, normal-map mip behaviour and roughness before increasing resolution or enabling expensive AA. An approved later performance profile can test MSAA separately.
6. **Texture handling is partly correct, with quality limits.** Importer explicitly marks normal maps as NormalMap, repacks glTF green roughness/blue metal into Unity alpha smoothness/red metal and sets packed maps nonsRGB. Base-colour images use normal colour import. It also compresses/resizes GLB images to 1024 before repacking material data, ignores authored sampler settings/secondary UV/texture transforms, and retains no general occlusion-map support. Only fix an unsupported feature where an audited donor uses it; do not claim every unsupported glTF extension damaged these cars. The city roughness loss is already proven independently of generic importer capability.

## Finite restoration sequence and comparison packet

Keep all changes in View/Editor/art authoring and preserve the pure Sim contract, recorded poses, physics contact surface and donor bytes. Freeze the current r9 stills and failed packets. Each experiment needs a unique candidate identity and recorded settings; this report author should not implement the candidate later judged by this author.

1. **P1 street slice:** restore the exact native Mercer corner geometry, authored static shading and full surface maps. Compare same camera, FOV, car, pose, sky heading and time. Show albedo-only, vertex colour-only and fully lit outputs. Acceptance is native-relative material/detail retention without doubled static/realtime shadows.
2. **P1 reflection/paint control:** same parked Catrina and Nocturne in a matched studio and on that corner, three-quarter and broadside. A/B only cube filtering/orientation first; then coat/normal treatment. Include grey, chrome and roughness spheres. Preserve broad readable car gradients, trim separation and donor paint identity.
3. **P1 light/output control:** fixed sunlit/shaded corner with neutral and saturated patches. A/B only output transform, then only light balance. Save HDR pre-grade diagnostics plus the final PNG. Confirm no premature clip and no unintentional duplicate colour encoding. Compare bright trim and blue-paint shadow detail by eye.
4. **P1 contact control:** four tyres down, Catrina three-wheel and one lifted front wheel; same pose and camera. A/B static city shade and car-local realtime shadow independently. Inspect tyre contact, chassis occlusion, building-shadow continuity and absence of surface acne. Use the existing guarded 960 x 600 profile unless a separate profile is authorized.
5. **P2 material/edge control:** front and grazing-angle glazing; foliage; wheel/roof/sign detail, followed by one short slow camera move. A/B foliage channel and shader, then material highlight filtering, then optional approved AA. Preserve all failed controls as diagnostic evidence.

After each accepted visual step, run the existing simulation/loaded-rig regression and compare fingerprints/replays. Measure same-machine draw calls, median/p99 and external peak memory against the preserved control within the permitted profile. A capped 960 x 600 run remains inconclusive for 1080p/60 or handheld acceptance. A fresh evaluator must compare the eventual candidate directly with the native reference and r9, without inheriting earlier scores. No visual PASS or paid-release readiness is established by this reassessment.
